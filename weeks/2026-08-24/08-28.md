## 2. Contrapositive vs. contradiction — the actual gap, not just the technique

**Prove:** if $n^2$ is odd, then $n$ is odd. Use the contrapositive.

Then, separately and explicitly: **why does proving "$n$ even $\Rightarrow n^2$ even"
establish the original claim** ("$n^2$ odd $\Rightarrow n$ odd")? Write the logical
relationship between the two implications — this is the part that's been missing both
times this has come up (08-19, 08-21): the technique gets named, but not *why* it's
valid.

## 3. Plain-English quantifier meaning — third exposure, fresh predicate

Let $P(x,y)$ be "$x+y=0$" over $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

**(a)** True or false, no proof needed: $\forall x\,\exists y\,P(x,y)$?
$\exists y\,\forall x\,P(x,y)$?

**(b)** In a sentence each: what does $\forall x\,\exists y\,P(x,y)$ **claim about the
real numbers**? (What is $y$, in words — can it depend on $x$, and if so how?) What
would $\exists y\,\forall x\,P(x,y)$ **have required** — what single number would $y$
have to be, and why does no real number work?

*(This is the same shape of question as 08-27's R2, deliberately — that one never got
attempted, so this is its real first cold try. If it comes back wrong or blank again,
this becomes a dedicated repair item next week rather than a fourth re-offer.)*
