## Core (~35 min)

### 1. Injective / surjective — this is the priority today

*(Calibration A4, then Session 2 Core 2 — blank both times. Not optional this time.)*

**(a)** Give a function $f : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ that is **injective but not
surjective.** Prove injectivity from the definition ($f(a)=f(b) \implies a=b$). Prove
non-surjectivity by exhibiting one specific integer that nothing maps to, and showing
— not just asserting — that nothing maps to it.

**(b)** Explain why **no such function can exist** when the domain and codomain are the
same *finite* set. Informal but precise is fine.

**(c)** One sentence: what does the contrast between (a) and (b) say about the
difference between finite and infinite sets?

### 2. Contradiction, and reusing a fact you almost proved

**(a)** Prove: if $n$ is an integer and $3 \mid n^2$, then $3 \mid n$.

*(This is yesterday's stretch problem, asked again because you need it next.)* Use the
contrapositive: assume $3 \nmid n$, so $n = 3k+1$ or $n = 3k+2$ for some
$k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Handle both cases and show $3 \nmid n^2$ in each.

**(b)** Use part (a) to prove **$\sqrt{3}$ is irrational**, by contradiction.

Structure: suppose $\sqrt3 = p/q$ in lowest terms ($p,q \in \mathbb{Z}$, $q\neq0$,
$\gcd(p,q)=1$). Get $p^2 = 3q^2$. Use (a) to conclude $3 \mid p$. Substitute
$p=3m$ and grind out the same move for $q$. Land on the contradiction.

This mirrors the $\sqrt2$ argument in lesson §4 — same skeleton, different prime —
but don't just pattern-match the lesson's proof with 2 replaced by 3. Write your own
lowest-terms argument and use *your* lemma from (a).

## Stretch (~15 min) — optional, fine to skip

**Prove that for any prime $p$, $\sqrt{p}$ is irrational.**

Same skeleton as Core 2, one level more general. You may cite **Euclid's lemma**
without proving it: if $p$ is prime and $p \mid ab$, then $p \mid a$ or $p \mid b$.
(Applying it to $a=b=n$ gives exactly the "$p \mid n^2 \implies p \mid n$" step that
Core 2(a) ground out by hand for $p=3$.)
