## Core (~35 min)

### 1. Unfolding drill — three short proofs

Each of these is 3–5 lines. **Structure every one the same way**, and label the parts
the first time through:

> *Setup* — name what you're assuming · *Unfold* — replace each defined word with its
> definition · *Work* — compute · *Land* — say why the result matches the claim

**(a)** Prove that the sum of two even integers is even.

**(b)** Prove that the product of two odd integers is odd.

**(c)** Prove that if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid c$, then $a \mid c$.

For (c), note you have *two* hypotheses, so you unfold twice and get two different
integers — name them different letters. Watch what happens if you call them both $k$.

### 2. Injective, surjective, and why finiteness matters

*(This is calibration A4, which you left blank.)*

**(a)** Give a function $f : \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ that is **injective but not
surjective.** Prove both claims — injectivity by the definition ($f(a) = f(b) \implies
a = b$), non-surjectivity by exhibiting a specific integer nothing maps to and showing
nothing maps to it.

**(b)** Now explain why **no such function can exist** when the domain and codomain are
the same *finite* set. An informal but precise argument is fine — you're not expected
to produce a formal pigeonhole proof yet. Say what goes wrong.

**(c)** One sentence: what does the contrast between (a) and (b) tell you about the
difference between finite and infinite sets?

## Stretch (~15 min) — optional, fine to skip

**Prove that if $n^2$ is divisible by 3, then $n$ is divisible by 3.**

Harder than R1 and worth knowing why: with 2 there are two cases (even/odd), but every
integer is one of $3k$, $3k+1$, or $3k+2$ — so the contrapositive needs **three cases**
and you have to check each. Take the contrapositive and grind it out.

*(If you get this, you've proved the lemma that makes $\sqrt{3}$ irrational by exactly
the §4 argument.)*
