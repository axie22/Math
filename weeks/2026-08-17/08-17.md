# Day 6 — Linear Algebra II (Foundational+)

📖 Lesson: [Linear Algebra II](../../lessons/linear-algebra-ii.md) — read this first if you haven't covered eigenvalues/eigenvectors, diagonalization, or SVD yet. Today draws on Sections 2–4.

## 1. Warm-up — eigendecomposition of a symmetric matrix

Let

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $A$ by hand (characteristic polynomial → null space). Verify explicitly that the two eigenvectors are orthogonal, and explain *why* the spectral theorem guarantees this in advance without you having to check.

## 2. Core — diagonalization to evolve a linear system

A simple two-sensor system (think: two coupled readings feeding into a control loop) evolves step-by-step as $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = A\mathbf{x}_k$, where

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{x}_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Diagonalize $A$ as $A = PDP^{-1}$, then use $A^4 = PD^4P^{-1}$ to compute $\mathbf{x}_4 = A^4\mathbf{x}_0$ *without* multiplying $A$ by itself four times. Then look at the ratio of the two components of $\mathbf{x}_4$ compared to $\mathbf{x}_0$, and explain what's happening in terms of the dominant eigenvalue/eigenvector — what would $\mathbf{x}_k$ look like as $k \to \infty$, and why?

## 3. Stretch — computing an SVD from scratch

Let

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.$$

$A$ is not symmetric, so it doesn't have an orthogonal eigendecomposition — but it still has an SVD. Compute $A^\top A$, find its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and use them to derive $\Sigma$, $V$, and $U$ in $A = U\Sigma V^\top$. (Use the identity from the lesson: $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(A^\top A)}$ and $\mathbf{u}_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_i}A\mathbf{v}_i$.)
