## 2. Synthesis (~30 min) — quantifier negation meets supremum

*Connects §5 (quantifier negation — retired 2026-08-25, the most solid skill in
the repo) to §9 (sup/inf — taught 2026-09-02, zero real attempts across seven
sessions and five distinct framings). Not a sup computation cold — a way into
one, using a tool that's already demonstrated.*

Recall part 2 of $s=\sup A$'s definition:
$$\forall \varepsilon>0\ \exists a\in A\ \big(a>s-\varepsilon\big).$$

**(a)** Write the negation of that statement — quantifiers flip, the domain
restriction $\varepsilon>0$ does **not** change, the final inequality negates.
Use §5's algorithm explicitly; don't just guess the shape.

**(b)** In one or two sentences: what does the statement you just wrote *mean*?
Why would it, if true of some candidate $s$, prove that $s$ is **not** the
least upper bound of $A$?

**(c)** Apply it. Let $A=\{1,2,3\}$ and consider the candidate $s=5$. Using the
negation from (a), find the specific $\varepsilon$ that witnesses $5\ne\sup A$
— i.e. show there's no $a\in A$ with $a>5-\varepsilon$ for that $\varepsilon$.
(There are many valid choices; one is enough.)

**(d)** Now go the other direction and build a real proof from scratch: prove
directly, from the two-part definition, that
$$\sup\left\{3-\dfrac1n : n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}=3.$$
