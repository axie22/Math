# Day 2 — Multivariable Calculus (foundational, building on gradients)

Yesterday you found gradients. Today: what the gradient actually *tells you* about a direction.

Let

$$f(x,y) = x^2y - 3xy^2 + 2y^3$$

**a)** Compute $\nabla f(2,1)$.

**b)** Let $\mathbf{v} = (3,4)$. Find the directional derivative of $f$ at $(2,1)$ in the direction of $\mathbf{v}$, i.e. $D_{\mathbf{u}}f(2,1)$ where $\mathbf{u}$ is the unit vector in the direction of $\mathbf{v}$.

**c)** What unit vector gives the direction of *steepest ascent* of $f$ at $(2,1)$, and what is the rate of increase in that direction? What about steepest *descent*?
