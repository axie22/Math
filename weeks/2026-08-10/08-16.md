# Day 5 — Linear Algebra II (Foundational)

📖 Lesson: [Linear Algebra II](../../lessons/linear-algebra-ii.md) — read this first if you haven't covered eigenvalues/eigenvectors yet.

New topic today! We're leaving multivariable calculus (for now — it'll come back once we start blending topics) and starting eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and matrix decompositions. Problem 1 deliberately reuses last week's Hessian test to bridge into this topic.

**1. (Warm-up — Hessian, reinforcing last week)** Let $f(x,y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3x - 3y + 4$.
Find the critical point of $f$, compute its Hessian matrix there, and use the second-derivative test to classify the critical point (local min, local max, or saddle).

**2. (Core — eigenvalues and eigenvectors)** Take the Hessian matrix $A$ you computed in Problem 1 (it doesn't depend on $(x,y)$ since $f$ is quadratic). Find its eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors directly from the characteristic polynomial $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$. Verify $A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}$ for each eigenpair, and explain how the *signs* of the eigenvalues you find are consistent with your classification in Problem 1.

**3. (Stretch — PCA flavor)** Suppose two features of a small dataset have (already-centered) covariance matrix
$$\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $\Sigma$ (these are the principal components). Which direction captures more variance, and what fraction of the *total* variance does the first principal component capture?
