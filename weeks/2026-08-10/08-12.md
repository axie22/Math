# Day 3 — Multivariable Calculus (intermediate: Hessian & second derivative test)

📖 Lesson: [Multivariable Calculus](../../lessons/multivariable-calculus.md) — read Section 3 first if you haven't seen the Hessian / second derivative test yet.

**1. Warm-up (reinforcing directional derivatives).**
Let $g(x,y) = x^2 + 2xy - y^2$. Find $\nabla g(2,-1)$, and compute the directional derivative of $g$ at $(2,-1)$ in the direction of $\mathbf{v} = (1,-1)$.

**2. Core (Hessian & second derivative test).**
Let $f(x,y) = x^3 - 3xy + y^3$.

a) Find all critical points of $f$.

b) Compute the Hessian at each critical point and use the second derivative test to classify it (local min, local max, or saddle).

**3. Stretch (loss-landscape flavor).**
Consider the function $L(w_1, w_2) = w_1^4 - 4w_1^2 + w_2^2$, a toy stand-in for a neural network loss with a "double-well" shape in $w_1$.

a) Find all critical points of $L$.

b) Classify each one using the Hessian.

c) In one or two sentences: why might a saddle point like the one you find here be more troublesome for gradient descent than a true local maximum?
