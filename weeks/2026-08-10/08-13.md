# Day 4 — Multivariable Calculus (advanced)

📖 Lesson: [Multivariable Calculus](../../lessons/multivariable-calculus.md) — Section 4 (Lagrange multipliers) is new today; read it first if you haven't seen constrained optimization before.

Today we add a constraint to the optimization toolkit: instead of finding extrema anywhere on the plane, we find them *restricted to a curve*. This is Lagrange multipliers, and it's the direct ancestor of SVM duality and of minimum-fuel control allocation in aerospace.

1. **Warm-up.** Find all critical points of $f(x,y) = x^2 - 4x + y^2 + 2y + 5$ and use the Hessian / second derivative test to classify each one. What is the minimum value of $f$?

2. **Core.** Use Lagrange multipliers to minimize $f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2$ subject to the constraint $x + y = 6$. Find the optimal point, the multiplier $\lambda$, and the minimum value.

3. **Stretch.** A small rocket has three attitude-control thrusters producing thrust $u_1, u_2, u_3$ (in Newtons). The total commanded thrust must sum to $90\text{ N}$: $u_1+u_2+u_3=90$. To minimize wasted power (proportional to the sum of squared thrusts), find the allocation $(u_1,u_2,u_3)$ that minimizes $f(u_1,u_2,u_3) = u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2$ subject to that constraint, using Lagrange multipliers with three variables. What is the minimum energy value, and what does the *shape* of the optimal allocation tell you about load-sharing across identical thrusters?
