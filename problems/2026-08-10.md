# Day 1 — Multivariable Calculus (foundational)

**Topic:** Partial derivatives and the gradient

**Problem:**

Let $f(x, y) = 3x^2y - 2xy^3 + 5$.

1. Compute the partial derivatives $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$.
2. Evaluate the gradient $\nabla f$ at the point $(1, 2)$.

---

**Solution:**

**Step 1 — Partial derivative with respect to $x$** (treat $y$ as a constant):

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 6xy - 2y^3$$

**Step 2 — Partial derivative with respect to $y$** (treat $x$ as a constant):

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 3x^2 - 6xy^2$$

**Step 3 — Evaluate at $(1, 2)$:**

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(1,2) = 6(1)(2) - 2(2)^3 = 12 - 16 = -4$$

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(1,2) = 3(1)^2 - 6(1)(2)^2 = 3 - 24 = -21$$

**Final answer:**

$$\nabla f(1,2) = (-4, -21)$$

Nice work — the gradient tells you the direction of steepest ascent of $f$ at that point. This idea is exactly what powers gradient descent in ML!
